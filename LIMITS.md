# Known limits

Every known limit of Strathe in one place, from a sweep of the code, its docs
and the research log. Each entry says what the limit means for a lab and what
to do about it. When a limit is lifted, it moves to the "Lifted" list at the
end, with the finding that lifted it. `PROJECT_STATUS.md #N` points to the
evidence.

Quick answers:
- **Can I use my own vehicle?** Yes: calibrate it from its logs, then use it in
  one- and two-vehicle experiments ([Your vehicle](#your-vehicle-and-its-thrusters)).
- **Are my log formats supported?** CSV, ROS 1/2 bags, ArduPilot `.bin` and
  MAVLink `.tlog` are read directly ([Logs](#logs-and-data-formats)).
- **Is it validated on hardware?** Not yet. Everything is simulation
  checked against held-out real logs ([Validation](#validation)).
- **Can the simulation be realistic rather than perfect?** Yes: `--mode noisy`
  adds sensor noise and roll/pitch disturbance, `--mode both` runs clean and
  noisy side by side, and `--buoyancy` changes the trim
  ([Clean vs noisy](#clean-vs-noisy-simulation-and-buoyancy)).
- **Can the fleet use an optical link?** Yes: `--link optical | hybrid`, with water
  clarity and terminal pointing, but the optical model is uncalibrated
  ([Communication model](#communication-model)).

## Validation

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **No hardware-in-the-loop validation yet** | Results are simulation. Calibration is checked on recording days it never saw, not on a live vehicle. | Run the same commands on the vehicle and in the calibrated simulator and compare. That's the partnership we're looking for. |
| **"calibrated" in fleet results means "a model equal to the simulated vehicle"** | The fleet studies show the value of an accurate teammate model. How close a real calibration gets to a real vehicle is measured separately, per axis, in the calibration report. | Read both reports together. |
| **Validation compares with the logs, not an independent position fix** | `validation/` (#65) measures position drift against the logged velocity integrated with the logged orientation, so it is the drift velocity error alone causes. Errors in the logged velocity itself (DVL drift) don't show up. | If your logs have an independent position (motion capture, USBL), compare the simulated path with it yourself; `validation.json` and the plots are the starting point. |
| **HoloOcean results are provisional** | A frame fix and a tick-rate guard are unconfirmed in HoloOcean itself; it doesn't run on macOS (#40). MuJoCo and the stand-in agree with each other. | Run `verify_holoocean_frame_conventions.py` on a HoloOcean machine before relying on HoloOcean numbers. |

## Logs and data formats

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Thruster commands: ESC PWM or normalized** | Commands logged in microseconds (1100–1900) work as they are. Normalized commands (e.g. −1..1, 0..1) need `command: normalized` and `command_range:` in the thrusters block (#67). | Give your command-to-thrust table in the logged units as `curve_csv` (`templates/thrust_curve_normalized_template.csv`); without it the T200 curve is used with the range mapped onto 1100–1900 µs. |
| **Supported formats: CSV, ROS 1 `.bag`, ROS 2 `.mcap`/`.db3`, ArduPilot DataFlash `.bin`/`.log`, MAVLink `.tlog`, PX4 `.ulg`, MOOS-IvP `.alog`, LCM** | Other formats aren't read directly: DUNE/IMC, vendor DVL binaries, spreadsheets. The MOOS-IvP and LCM readers are proven on real recordings written into those formats, not yet on a lab's own log (#94). LCM needs `pip install lcm`, which has no wheel for Intel Macs. | Export other formats to CSV (one row per sample, any column names) and use `templates/ingest_csv_*.yaml`. If a MOOS-IvP or LCM log misbehaves, send it: `calibration/LOG_FORMATS.md`. |
| **Older ROS 2 `.db3` bags (before Iron) with custom messages** | They don't store message definitions, so custom types like `mavros_msgs/RCOut` can't be decoded on their own. | List the `.msg` files under `source.message_types` (templates README). ROS 1 bags and `.mcap` bags are fine. |
| **Body-frame velocity is required** | Calibration needs velocity (surge/sway/heave) from a DVL, visual odometry or a positioning system. World-frame velocity works too (`linear_frame: world`). | ArduSub's EKF velocity *without* a DVL or positioning system is dead reckoning and won't calibrate anything useful. |
| **ArduPilot `.bin` without GPS has no calendar date** | Underwater logs usually have no GPS, so their clock is time since boot. Calibration splits recordings by date to test on days it never saw. | Use `session_date: {from: filename}` or `{from: constant, value: ...}`. `.tlog` files carry the ground station's clock. |
| **MAVLink telemetry is low-rate by default** | Ground stations stream ATTITUDE/SERVO_OUTPUT_RAW at a few Hz unless told otherwise. | Raise the stream rates to at least 10 Hz before recording (template comments), and set `resample_hz` no higher. |
| **Re-recorded ROS bags** | A bag that was replayed and re-recorded carries the re-recording's receive times. Topics with header stamps keep the mission's clock; topics without them are moved onto it by the median delay between the two, so everything lines up and `session_date: {from: recording}` gives the mission's date (#91, URI's bags re-recorded 10 months later). With `stamp: receive`, times and dates are the re-recording's, and ingestion warns. A re-recorded bag with no header stamps at all can't recover the mission's clock. | Keep `stamp: header` (the default). If no topic has header stamps, use `session_date: {from: filename}` or `{from: constant, ...}`. |
| **Orientation glitches** | Real IMU logs contain sudden jumps (the reference data has 239 jumps over 20° within 0.1 s). Ingestion slerps across them and warns about jumps over 45°. | Check the warnings in `ingest_report.json`; trim or drop recordings with many. |
| **One vehicle per ingestion spec, one sampling rate per calibration** | All files converted together must end up at one rate. | Set `resample_hz`. Use separate specs for separate vehicles. |
| **One table per recording (CSV)** | CSV ingestion reads one file per recording with every column in it. Logs exported as one CSV per sensor, at different rates, have to be merged first. | Merge them onto one time base; `calibration/examples/dfki_tether/` does it for a public dataset (motion capture + ArduSub telemetry, #62). |
| **One session is an easier check** | With one recording date, calibration holds out recordings within the day; with one recording, its last 30% after a 10 s gap (#93). Those share the session's trim, battery and water, so the check can't show how another day changes them. On the real data a one-session score stayed within 0.07 of the same model's score on sessions it never saw (BlueROV2 tank days: +0.04 to +0.05 and once -0.04; ALPHA RISE open-water missions: -0.06 and -0.07). The report says so at the top. A recording under about 3.5 minutes can't be split. | Record a second session, even a short one, for the honest number ([calibration/ONE_SESSION.md](calibration/ONE_SESSION.md)). |

## Your vehicle and its thrusters

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **One built-in preset: BlueROV2 Heavy** | Any other vehicle is described once with a `layout:`: position, direction and reversed flag per thruster. There is no one-line preset for, e.g., the standard 6-thruster BlueROV2. | Copy `templates/ingest_csv_pwm_custom_thrusters.yaml` and edit the layout; `examples/dfki_tether/ingest_dfki_tether.yaml` is a standard 6-thruster BlueROV2. Vehicles that can't push every axis are supported. |
| **The default vehicle description is a BlueROV2 Heavy** | Without `--spec`, the calibration starts from an 11.2 kg BlueROV2's mass, inertia, drag and limits. On another vehicle that fits poorly: URI's 33 kg AUV calibrated with it had no trustworthy axis and drifted 70 m a minute, against 3.5 m with its own description (#70). `calibrate` warns when the logs' thrusters aren't a BlueROV2 Heavy's, and the report says so. | Write one with `strathe describe-vehicle` from your mass and size, or from your simulator model (`calibration/DESCRIBE_VEHICLE.md`), and pass it with `--spec` to every command (LAB_GUIDE.md step 4). |
| **The shipped reference calibration is a mirror image** | The reference dataset is in ROS axes (forward-left-up, z up) and was calibrated as it is. Its model is the same vehicle turned half a turn about x: identical for every experiment, except that its fitted net buoyancy and surge-pitch coupling (`Xq_dot`) have the opposite sign to the real vehicle's, and with `--thrusters bluerov2_heavy` the vertical thrusters' stronger direction (47.9 vs 37.2 N each) is reversed relative to gravity (#62). | Nothing for formation or station-keeping experiments. Don't quote the reference calibration's net buoyancy as the real vehicle's. Your own calibration, from logs converted at ingestion, is in FRD/NED. |
| **`describe-vehicle` sizes the limits from the hull it's given** | Drag, added mass and inertia limits scale with the length, width/height or diameter. From `--from-model` those come from the model's collision shapes, which may not be the hull: bluerov2_gz's is a 5 cm buoyancy block, so the BlueROV2's limits came out too small and held-out skill fell from 0.379 (hand-written spec) to 0.312, sway from 0.51 to 0.21; with its height given, 0.434. URI's ALPHA RISE in Gazebo has a 0.17 m cylinder sized to displace its 33 kg (the hull is 0.254 m): added-mass limits 57% too small, pinned by the fit (#95). Both cases print a note. | Read the notes. Measure the outer hull and give `--length` with `--width`/`--height` or `--diameter` alongside `--from-model`; the model still supplies mass and inertia. |
| **The thruster model isn't calibrated** | The PWM → thrust curve and the layout are taken as given (T200 data, or your curve file). Errors in them become errors in the calibrated drag. Voltage rescaling (`voltage:`, `voltage_column:`) uses Blue Robotics' published T200 maxima, so it's for T200 thrusters; a lab table is rescaled from its `curve_voltage:` by that ratio, assuming its shape doesn't change with voltage (#69). | Use a bollard-pull curve at your battery voltage if you have one (`curve_csv`, with `curve_voltage:`), and log the battery voltage (`voltage_column:`). Other thrusters: measure the curve at the voltage you run at. |
| **Thruster lag is one time constant, and opt-in** | Without it, thrust follows the command instantly, and the simulated vehicle responds faster than the real one: on the reference BlueROV2, 0.3 s to 63% of a step against the real 0.6 s (`validation/`, #65). `thruster_time_constant_s` (#66) lags every thruster the same way. On the reference vehicle a full refit with it fits 0.34 s: step timing matches, drift and roll/pitch improve, yaw is 0.025 worse, and the logs can't separate lag from inertia, so yaw inertia and added mass go to their bounds (#66). On DFKI the fit finds none. Battery sag is handled when the voltage is logged (`voltage_column:`, #63). | Add the `thruster_dynamics` section (commented out in `vehicle_spec.yaml`) to fit it, and compare the validation step responses with and without it. Check the report's "stuck at a limit" list: if inertia or added mass hit their bounds, pin them from CAD or a pendulum test, or measure your thrusters' spin-up on a bench. Log the battery voltage and use `voltage_column:`. |
| **The thruster-limit mixer isn't ArduSub's exact mixer** | In the simulators a command is split over the thrusters by least squares and scaled down as a whole if any thruster saturates. That matches ArduSub's direction-preserving behaviour, not its exact code. | Fine for experiments; don't expect saturation to match the vehicle to the newton. |
| **Default action bound is ±30 N / N·m per axis without thruster limits** | Without `thrusters:`, any policy command up to 30 on every axis is delivered, including ~30 N·m of pitch torque a BlueROV2 Heavy can't make (~18 N·m). | Turn on `thrusters:` (`--thrusters`) for realistic authority. |

## Calibration (the physics model and the fit)

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Roll and pitch aren't predictive on the reference data** | After the torque-arm fix they are at about zero skill: no better than "no motion" (#50). The shipped roll/pitch damping sits at its limits and isn't the vehicle's: the logs' own free decays measure 20–50× less, and predict free swings on unseen days well (#64). With that real damping, the model's thruster-driven roll/pitch is far too large, so whole-mission prediction gets worse (−3.7), and the heavy damping is what hides it. This doesn't affect formation-holding (#52). | For attitude-sensitive work: `strathe free-decay` on free-decay tank tests (FREE_DECAY_PROTOCOL.md) measures the unforced swing; `calibrate --free-decay` holds it (opt-in; compare with a calibration made without it). Deliberate roll/pitch commands with the tether slack are what the thruster-driven part needs; Strathe doesn't fit them yet. `--exclude-axes p q` leaves roll and pitch out of the fit. |
| **Many parameters end at their allowed limits** | On the reference data, 8–15 of 22 parameters sit at `vehicle_spec.yaml` bounds (#50); on DFKI's, 11 (#62). Logged motion rarely separates linear from quadratic drag (correlations −0.90 to −0.99), so the fit slides along that trade-off to a bound. The report lists them in plain words. | Review the bounds with someone who knows the vehicle. `--drag prior` (opt-in, #63) pulls the drag gently toward published BlueROV2 values (Wu 2018, literature-derived, not measured, for the standard 6-thruster vehicle); it helped a little on DFKI and changed nothing on the reference dataset, where the fit ends at the same limits (#63). Holding the linear terms at those values made the fit unstable, so that isn't offered. On a vehicle that isn't a BlueROV2, first check its vehicle description is its own (step 4 of LAB_GUIDE.md); `--drag prior` is BlueROV2-only (it hurt on URI's torpedo AUV, #70). If a widened limit is hit again, the model is missing physics for that vehicle (on a torpedo hull: lift and pitching moment at speed), not range (#70). |
| **A tether that goes taut is learned as drag** | Calibration has no tether model. On DFKI's limited-tether runs the cable went taut in bursts of tens of newtons for 20–40% of the time; trained on those, surge drag ran to its bounds and surge prediction on free runs was no better than the default model (#63). | Record free-swimming sessions with the tether slack. If you log tether tension (or any flag for bad stretches), `exclude_samples:` in the ingest spec drops them. |
| **Some parameters can't be told apart** | E.g. linear vs quadratic drag on the same axis. The report's identifiability section names the pairs and groups. | Treat those individual values as uncertain. Manoeuvres at varied speeds while turning separate them. |
| **Model structure** | Diagonal added mass, no payload changes, no off-diagonal added-mass coupling (`vehicle_spec.yaml` assumptions). Box-frame vehicles: a centre-of-gravity offset in z only, no lift. Torpedo-shaped hulls can add hull lift, its moment, pitch/yaw damping that grows with speed and a fore-aft centre-of-gravity offset (`hull_lift:` and `cg_cb_offset_x` in `templates/vehicle_spec_template.yaml`, #71), one set for both planes (an axisymmetric hull). No fins or control surfaces yet. | These are the model's assumptions; they are listed in the vehicle description. A finned vehicle's fins are lumped into the hull terms. |
| **Teacher-forced orientation during calibration** | The fit replays logged roll/pitch rather than integrating them (`dynamics_model.py`). Its error isn't directly comparable to a free-running simulator's. | The report's per-axis skill is measured the same way for every model, so comparisons within Strathe are fair. |
| **Explicit Euler integration** | The physics uses small sub-steps (0.005 s). An unusual vehicle could need smaller ones. | If a simulation raises `FloatingPointError`, lower `physics_dt` before blaming the parameters. |
| **Budget** | A full fit on a large log set takes about an hour (7 cores). If the evaluation budget runs out, the best result so far is kept and marked "not converged". Values inside the limits that make the simulation blow up (NaN/inf) on a recording are treated as a very bad fit, so the optimizer moves away from them instead of stopping (#78; before, URI's surfacings-removed + widened-limits calibration stopped after 3 minutes). If a fit still stops on one, it says so and keeps the best result the same way (#76). | Re-run with a larger `--max-nfev` for a converged fit. If the optimizer keeps finding unstable values, narrow the limits the report lists at their edges. |
| **The result can depend on where the fit starts** | The fit starts from a randomly perturbed point. A start that drifts to values where the vehicle is barely damped makes the long replay chaotic, and the fit stalls or wanders (#93). Strathe now catches that (a fit no better than its starting description, or one that ended on a rough cost) and tries up to 3 more starting points by itself; a fit that only ran out of budget on a smooth cost is finished from its best point instead, since it was still improving. An easy calibration lands in the same place every time (URI's baseline: 7 runs, each direction within 0.001 of mean skill); a hard one can land in different minima (URI with surfacings removed: +0.06 or +0.24 mean skill, 10.6 or 4.7 m of drift after 60 s, #76). Each calibration records its seed in `calibrated_parameters.yaml` and the report. On URI's data, the start with the lower training cost was the better one on unseen recordings every time (#79). | `strathe calibrate ... --restarts 3` starts from three points and keeps the lowest training cost; the report lists each start's cost. With `--estimate-current` that's the first round only, and the best round-1 start isn't always the best at the end (URI's B8, #79): `--restart-whole-fit` runs every round from each start and keeps the lowest final training cost. `--seed N` repeats a calibration exactly. |
| **Long runs need the machine awake** | Long runs keep the machine awake themselves (#49), but closing a laptop lid still sleeps it. | Leave the lid open, or run on a desktop or server. |

## Water current

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **One constant current per recording** | Calibration, and current estimation, assume the current is the same throughout each recording. A current that changes within a recording, or differs from place to place, isn't modelled. | Keep recordings short enough (a few minutes) that the current is steady. `estimate-current` checks each recording by estimating its two halves separately, and flags ones where they disagree. |
| **A recording the model can't simulate gets no current** | If the parameters make the simulation blow up on a recording, current estimation holds that recording at still water and flags it ("no current estimated"), and the others carry on; it used to stop the whole calibration (#76). | Treat a flagged recording as a sign the parameters are unstable for its motion: check the calibration's limits. |
| **Estimating an unknown current needs a decent vehicle model and some turning** | With uncalibrated default physics the estimates are unreliable, and the tool says so ("explains only N% of the error"). A vehicle that never turns can't separate a current from drag along its heading. | Use `strathe calibrate --estimate-current`, which alternates fitting and estimating. Include turns in open-water recordings. |
| **Simulated currents are uniform in space** | In experiments the current can vary in time (slow fluctuation), and it's the same everywhere. No shear or eddies; both fleet vehicles feel the same water. | Enough for station-keeping and formation studies over tens of metres. |
| **Policies don't observe the current** | They see their own velocity over ground, like a DVL on bottom track. | Pass the mean current to physics teammate models with `known_to_beliefs`. |

## Clean vs noisy simulation, and buoyancy

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **The sensor model is simple** | White noise, a constant bias per episode, and DVL dropouts, per 0.1 s tick (`sensor_noise.py`). Not modelled: compass errors that depend on heading, gyro bias that wanders during a run, scale-factor errors, sensor latency, DVL altitude limits. | Enough to test whether a result survives realistic sensors. Measure your own sizes with `strathe fit-noise`. |
| **`typical` is assumed; `tank` and `open_water` are measured** | `tank` is the noise in the reference tank logs (small, with external positioning). `open_water` is measured from SINTEF's SOLAQUA data, a BlueROV2 at a fish farm (#60): white noise, DVL dropouts and the USBL starting-fix error. Its biases are `typical`'s assumed values. `typical` (DVL + AHRS + dead reckoning) uses stated sizes of a common magnitude, **not measured on any vehicle**, and is still the default of `--mode noisy`. Every report says which. The measured open-water white noise is 2–35× smaller than `typical`'s; the starting fix (0.17 m measured, 0.2 m assumed) is close. | For your vehicle, measure your own with `strathe fit-noise --data <logs>` and pass the file, adding datasheet biases. `--noise open_water` is the measured open-water reference until then. |
| **Biases can't be measured from logs** | A constant offset per dive is invisible without an independent reference, so `fit-noise` writes biases as 0 and says so. SOLAQUA has a USBL reference, but its DVL tracks the fish-farm net, which moves with the current, so it can't measure them either (#60). | Put your sensors' datasheet biases in the file. A bias needs a seabed-locked DVL logged with an independent position and heading reference. |
| **The starting position fix is most of what sensor noise costs the fleet** | Each vehicle starts dead reckoning from its own fix, with an independent error, so the relative position is off by about 1.4× the fix error against a ±0.2 m tolerance. With perfect sensors, a 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3 m fix costs 1 / 3 / 9 / 18 points; with a perfect fix, the `typical` or measured open-water sensors cost about 1 (#60). | Set `--start-fix-error` (or `sensor_noise.start_fix_error_m`) to your positioning's accuracy, and report it with any noisy result. Relative positioning between the vehicles (acoustic ranging) is the lever. |
| **`open_water`'s DVL tracked a net at 0.5–2 m** | In SOLAQUA the DVL locks onto the fish-farm net, not the seabed. A DVL at that range is quieter than one bottom-locked at 10–50 m, and gyro, roll/pitch and heading are ArduSub's filtered estimates, not raw IMU samples. | Treat `open_water`'s DVL noise as a lower bound for deep bottom-lock; `--noise-scale 2` brackets it. |
| **`fit-noise` can't measure noise in smoothed logs** | Logs filtered by the vehicle, or interpolated when converted from another rate, have their white noise smoothed away. `fit-noise` detects it: consecutive second differences correlate at −2/3 for white noise, and above −0.55 the channel is flagged, given the `typical` preset's value instead of almost zero, and named in a note (#91). The reference BlueROV2 logs and URI's resampled logs are both flagged. | Log raw sensor readings and convert at the sensors' native rate (`resample_hz`), or measure each stream at its own rate as `fit_solaqua_noise.py` does; otherwise use datasheet values. |
| **Navigation is raw dead reckoning or external positioning** | No mid-run USBL/LBL fixes and no navigation filter: the policy sees dead-reckoned position that drifts from a starting fix. | Use `position: external` (with its accuracy) for a vehicle with continuous positioning. |
| **Beacons carry each vehicle's believed position; no acoustic ranging** | Real modems can also measure the range between vehicles, which would cancel the two vehicles' independent starting-fix errors, the main cost of sensor noise here (#60). | Research: ranging-aided teammate models. |
| **A buoyancy change is a net force only** | `--buoyancy` changes the net buoyancy of both vehicles by the same amount. The centre of gravity, the righting moment and the mass don't change; nothing changes with depth. | Recalibrate after a large payload change. |
| **Policies can push back at most 30 N vertically without thruster limits** | Beyond that a policy can't hold depth; with `--thrusters` the real limit is lower. Values beyond ±50 N are refused as a probable units mistake. | |

## Single-vehicle arena (`strathe arena`)

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Training always uses the stand-in engine** | MuJoCo (`--backend mujoco` or `both`) is for testing; training on it would be ~3× slower. The two engines agree closely (#55, and the arena stress test). | Use `--backend both` to check that the results hold on both engines. |
| **Four tasks** | Reach a point, hold station, dock, avoid one obstacle. New tasks need a reward and a config in `arena.py`. | Ask us, or subclass `Task`. |
| **Episodes start level, at the origin, at rest** | Targets are random within ±3 m. | |
| **The fitted disturbance was fitted to the reference BlueROV2** | `--disturbance fitted` reproduces that vehicle's tether/wave roll/pitch wobble. On another vehicle it's a generic "rough water" setting. | Fit your own with `fit_rollpitch_disturbance.py`, then pass the file. |
| **SAC and TD3 use stable-baselines3's own settings** | `--algorithm sac\|td3` trains with one environment and SB3's defaults, the setup validated on this vehicle (#37). No hyperparameter tuning per vehicle or task. | Compare seeds; PPO remains the default. |
| **Training-seed variance** | Reinforcement learning results vary between seeds. A heavily damped vehicle is slow to learn on (10% vs 96% success at 100k vs 300k steps, #56). The arena's intervals resample training seeds as well as episodes, so they include this (#91). With one seed they can't, and the report says so. | Use several seeds (`--seeds 3` or more) and a longer `--timesteps`; read the per-seed differences in the report. |

## Fleet (two vehicles)

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Exactly two vehicles** | N ≠ 2 is rejected. The per-vehicle observation and shared policy are the groundwork for N. | |
| **Both vehicles are the same vehicle** | They share `truth_params`, `vehicle_spec` and `thrusters`. | Two different vehicles in one fleet isn't supported yet. |
| **One task: formation hold** | Hold a separation for a number of ticks without colliding. The defaults (2 m ± 0.2 m, 0.5 m collisions) are sized for a BlueROV2. | Separation, tolerance, hold time, episode length and collision radius are settable for testing (`strathe fleet --target-separation ...`) and for training (`strathe train-fleet --target-separation ... --separations MIN MAX`, #91). Train and test with the same geometry: the report warns when a policy was trained for another one. For the arena, `--target-radius` and `--target-range`. |
| **Start state** | Level, facing +x, on a line. Separation and closing speed are chosen per start. | `--separations`, `--closing-speeds`. |
| **Policies act on beacons plus a teammate model; no intent sharing** | Calibrated prediction ties "last beacon" in still water (#54). It pulls ahead in a current it knows about (#55). A beacon carries the teammate's state and the thrust it delivered on the send tick, never a planned command. An optical link (a beacon every tick) gives high-rate *state* sharing (#59), not intent sharing. | `--link hybrid` for high-rate state sharing. Intent sharing (planned commands or a trajectory in the beacon) is research, the open lever from #48. |
| **Policies trained for one teammate model vary across seeds** | Policies specialised to the calibrated model ranged 35–98% (#54). The mixed-trained policies are consistent. | Use `--belief mix` (the default) for policies you report. |
| **The shipped policies were trained on the reference BlueROV2** | They run on other vehicles, but results for your vehicle should come from policies trained on it. | `strathe train-fleet --params <your calibration>` (~40 min). |
| **SAC and TD3 fleet policies were validated on one task and one vehicle** | Three seeds each, 1M steps, passed criteria fixed in advance: SAC ties PPO (−0 points, 95% CI −1 to +1) and TD3 is 4 points better (+3 to +6) over 17,600 paired episodes (#58). That's formation hold on the reference BlueROV2, clean simulation, SB3-standard settings with no tuning for another vehicle. The ranking doesn't necessarily carry over: TD3 went from best in clean simulation to worst under noise (−22 points). | For your vehicle, train two or three seeds of each (`strathe train-fleet --params <yours> --algorithm sac --seed 1 ...`) and compare them in one experiment (`--policies ppo=a ppo=b sac=c sac=d`), ideally with `--mode both`. The report's "Policy groups compared" table gives the paired difference with a 95% CI. |
| **SAC and TD3 cost more per step** | Each off-policy step includes gradient updates from a replay buffer: about 500-650 steps/s on one M1 core vs about 770 for PPO. With a third of the steps, a 1M-step run takes about 30-35 min alone, against about 65 min for PPO's 3M. Several runs sharing a machine slow each other down (the study's own runs took 2-2.5 h with 9 at once). | Run fewer at a time than cores, as with PPO. |

## Your own simulator (Stonefish)

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Built-in simulators: Stonefish, Gazebo Harmonic, MuJoCo and HoloOcean (provisional)** | `fit-sim --sim stonefish` (and `fit-stonefish`), `--sim gazebo` (#82), `--sim mujoco` (#83) and `--sim holoocean` (#84; tested against a stand-in with HoloOcean's Fossen interface: the real HoloOcean check needs Windows or Linux) calibrate those models. MuJoCo can't take Strathe's model (`export-sim`): it has no per-axis linear damping, buoyancy force or full added mass. Gazebo's export is exact when the logs' reference point is the link's origin; elsewhere its quadratic damping can't be moved there (Gazebo has no term for it). Any other simulator works through `fit-sim --sim external`, once it has a replay command and a short YAML (#81). | Connect yours: [calibration/simulators/SIMULATOR_PROTOCOL.md](calibration/simulators/SIMULATOR_PROTOCOL.md), with a working example in `calibration/simulators/example/`. |
| **Added mass needs a Stonefish patch** | Stonefish averages a body's added mass over its three axes, which is wrong for a slender vehicle, and can't set it from a scenario. `calibration/stonefish/stonefish_added_mass.patch` (about 100 lines) adds both. Without the patch, `fit-stonefish` fits drag, buoyancy and the CG only. | Apply the patch to your Stonefish (`calibration/stonefish/README.md`). |
| **Values the logs can't pin down** | A fit can trade one axis for another, and can tailor a value to the training recordings: an early version made ALPHA RISE's surge added mass 172–274 kg (physically about 10) to fit pitch and yaw better (#72). Errors are now weighted by how much each axis varies (#73). | Read the fitted values in the report. Hold what you've measured with `--hold`, e.g. `added_mass_x=10.6`. |
| **One part, per-axis values, no hull lift** | The tuned part's drag and added mass are per axis. Stonefish derives rotational drag from the same coefficients over the mesh and has no hull-lift term, so the fit may raise sideways drag to stand in for lift. | Read the report's fitted values: odd ones mean the logs don't pin them down. |
| **Open-loop replay** | Each window replays the logged forces from the logged state, in still water, at a fixed depth. Over a closed-loop mission, "keep the starting velocity" beats every simulator; the scores rank models. | Compare the original and the calibrated model on the same windows, as the report does. |
| **Slow** | Every fitting step replays your recordings in Stonefish: an hour or more. | `--workers`; fewer recordings for a first look. |
| **Docker (nothing to build) is checked on a Mac only** | Stonefish and Gazebo in Docker gave the same results as local builds on a Mac (Colima), about 1.5× slower there (#92). Not yet run on Windows: Strathe translates `C:\` paths for the container, untested. The images have no display, so looking at a calibrated model still needs the simulator installed. | On Windows, run Strathe inside WSL 2 for now. Each Docker message and its fix: `calibration/simulators/DOCKER.md` section 5. |
| **Values converted with `export-stonefish` don't reproduce Strathe's model** | Stonefish's mesh drag grows with speed cubed and couples the axes: once the hull pitches a few degrees, its side faces add surge drag. Each converted value is exact (checked by reloading Stonefish), but on URI's ALPHA RISE the converted model's error after 10 s was 73–133% worse than the original's, while after 60 s it was 17–28% better (#74). Stonefish's rotational drag comes from the same coefficients, so Strathe's rotational damping can't be carried either. By axis: heave and heading improve; surge, roll and pitch worsen. The values balance against Strathe's own drag and righting: a transplanted CG − CB (3–5 mm against Stonefish's 97 mm) costs roll and pitch (#75). The report lists the axes and says this. | Build Stonefish with `calibration/stonefish/stonefish_added_mass.patch`: its per-axis damping is Strathe's law, so the export carries the damping as it is. The export also puts the CG at the logs' reference point, as Strathe's model has it (needs `--buoyancy-part`). On ALPHA RISE the speeds then matched Strathe's model and the error after 10 s fell 56–68% against the conversion; the bay beat Strathe's own model at every horizon, the pond's pitch still drifts more, so there `fit-stonefish` stays better over a minute (#80). Starting `fit-stonefish` from the converted values (`--from-strathe`) ended worse (#74). |

## Communication model

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Acoustic link: a distance ramp, a modem delay and travel time** | Loss ramps with distance; latency is a fixed modem delay plus travel time. No obstruction, multipath, collisions or message priorities (`comm_channel.py`). Without a link calibration, the named levels L0–L3b (0–60% loss, no modem delay) are assumed; `--modem-delay` and `--bandwidth-bytes` set the delay and the per-tick byte budget by hand. | Calibrate it from your modems' message logs: `strathe fit-link`, then `--link-calibration` (#68). Both vehicles' clocks must be synchronised for latency. |
| **Beacon size is nominal (24 bytes)** | It isn't a real modem framing format. | Matters only for the bandwidth-starved level L3b. |
| **The optical link is uncalibrated unless you calibrate it** | Delivery follows received light power `exp(-c·d)/d²` through a smooth S-curve whose midpoint is set by `rated_range_m` (50% delivery at 30 m in clear water by default), a placeholder. `strathe fit-link` fits the curve and latency to your logged messages (#68); pointing (angle) isn't fitted. Reports say which it is. | `strathe fit-link` on your optical modems' logs, then `--link-calibration`. Without logs, set `rated_range_m` and `data_rate_bps` from the datasheet. |
| **Water clarity values are textbook figures** | `clear` 0.15, `coastal` 0.40, `harbor` 2.2 per metre: classic measured attenuation coefficients as usually quoted, not your site. Turbidity doesn't change during a run. Message logs can't measure it either: `fit-link` holds it (coastal by default) and fits the delivery curve around it, which only matters beyond the logged ranges (#68). | `water: custom` with `attenuation_per_m` from a transmissometer at your site; list several waters to bracket it. |
| **Pointing is a hard cone on the true attitude** | Inside a terminal's half-angle the link is at full strength; outside it, nothing. The true roll, pitch and yaw decide it, so optical results are more sensitive to attitude than acoustic ones, and roll/pitch isn't predictive yet (#50). The shipped policies roll and pitch freely while holding formation, so narrow cones rarely stay pointed at the teammate (#59). | `terminals: omni` (the default) is the fair baseline. Use `compare_omni` to measure what pointing costs; train with `--link hybrid` to see whether a policy learns to keep its terminals pointed. |
| **Not modelled for light: occlusion, sunlight, bubbles, multipath** | The other vehicle's hull, the seabed or anything in between never blocks the beam; ambient light near the surface and bubbles don't degrade it. The same holds for acoustic occlusion. | Treat near-surface optical results as optimistic. |
| **Two vehicles, so no shared acoustic channel (`strathe fleet`)** | Acoustic energy and airtime cost nothing here, so hybrid `redundant` (every beacon on both links) is never worse than `optical_first`. The value of `optical_first` (saving acoustic energy and channel time) can't show up until three or more vehicles share a channel. | Report `redundant` for what the information is worth; `optical_first` for what a real link manager would do. |
| **`optical_first` decides the link is up from what it has heard** | A vehicle skips its acoustic beacon only if an optical message from its teammate arrived within the last `link_up_ticks` ticks (default 1: on the previous tick). Symmetric terminals make the geometry reciprocal at an instant, but each direction draws its losses independently, so a skipped acoustic beacon's optical copy can still be lost. The report counts those (3.2% of skips across the stress grid). With asymmetric terminals (beam ≠ receiver half-angle) the inference is unsafe, so it runs as `redundant` automatically, and the report says so. No acknowledgements are modelled. | Set `link_up_ticks` (YAML) to match your modem's link-status logic; wider windows skip more and lose more. |
| **No latency advantage for optical** | Messages are delivered on the 0.1 s tick: anything sent with any latency above zero arrives on the next tick. Optical's 1 ms and acoustic's few milliseconds at fleet ranges (under 150 m) are therefore identical in the simulation. Optical's advantage here is its send rate (every tick) and its loss, not its latency. | Don't read a latency effect into optical results. Acoustic latency starts to cost a tick beyond ~150 m. |
| **The shipped fleet policies never saw an optical link** | They were trained with acoustic beacons every 1–3 s. With omni terminals an optical or hybrid link lifts them to 100% in every loss condition. With 30° cones they lose the link as they roll, and fore/aft cones raise collisions: an early fix goes stale while they close (#59). What a policy trained with a link could do isn't measured yet. | `strathe train-fleet --link hybrid` (and the web form) trains with it. |


## Multi-vehicle coordination (`strathe comms-study`)

| Limit | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **The channel is a generic model** | Travel time, time on the air, modem delay, TDMA or random access, half duplex, collisions and bursty loss are modelled at textbook values (`acoustic_network.py`). The modem classes (`slow`, `medium`, `fast`) are generic, not products. | Set your modem's bit rate, preamble and delay; measure loss against range with `strathe fit-link`. |
| **Not modelled: multipath, Doppler, the capture effect, carrier sensing, acknowledgements, relaying, TDMA clock drift** | Two packets that overlap at a receiver are both lost (no "stronger one survives"); no retransmissions; every vehicle hears every other directly. TDMA slots are perfectly synchronised. | Treat random-access results as pessimistic and TDMA results as optimistic about timing. |
| **Navigation error is dead reckoning only, off by default** | `comms-study --nav-drift PCT --start-fix-error M` gives each vehicle a heading-reference error (drift as a % of distance) and a starting fix error; beacons carry what it believes. No acoustic ranging, USBL or LBL corrections bound them, and the drift is one fixed angle per vehicle, not a noise process. On the shipped fleet a 2 m fix error already breaks coordination (23%), while 5% drift doesn't (#89). | Set them to your navigation's accuracy, and report them with any result. Without them, the result assumes every vehicle knows exactly where it is. |
| **One task: following an unpredictable leader in formation** | Leader-follower with a formation that keeps its compass orientation (or turns with the leader, slowly enough to fly). No area coverage, rendezvous or task allocation. | Your own controller (`--controller module:Class`) can do any strategy within this task; the score is formation error and separation. |
| **The built-in controller is a baseline, not tuned** | A PID to the believed slot with velocity feed-forward and a simple repulsion. A better strategy can tolerate more. | Compare yours on the same episodes: `--controller yours --sweep controller ...`. |
| **The same vehicle for the whole fleet, full 6-DOF thrust** | Every vehicle uses `--params` and `--vehicle-spec`; thrust is limited per axis (±30 N, ±5 N·m), not by a thruster layout. | For an underactuated vehicle, write a controller that only uses the axes it has. |

## Lifted

What used to be a limit, and where it was fixed:
- **Logs in other formats (#56, #57):**
  - ROS bags are read directly (#56).
  - ArduPilot `.bin` and MAVLink `.tlog` are read directly (#57).
- **Custom thrusters needed hand-made matrix files (#56):** a `layout:` in the ingestion YAML now does it.
- **Battery sag ignored (#63):** `voltage_column:` scales each sample's thrust to its logged voltage; with a lab's own T200 table too, given its `curve_voltage:` (#69).
- **Reversed thrusters or a mirrored heading went unnoticed (#63):** ingestion checks force against velocity on every driven axis and refuses data that pushes the wrong way; it warns (doesn't refuse) when they barely move together, which is how a mirrored heading with world-frame velocity shows; and it checks orientation against positions when given with body-frame velocity.
- **The BlueROV2 Heavy preset was in ROS axes (#62):** it pushed sideways, vertically, in pitch and in yaw the wrong way for ArduSub logs. It is now in ArduSub's motor numbering and FRD, checked against ArduSub's motor table and against a second, independent BlueROV2 with motion capture.
- **`strathe calibrate` only worked on logs with the reference dataset's dates (#62):** the data check, the identifiability step and the report defaulted to its held-out dates; they now use the same split as calibration.
- **Water current:**
  - A known current is supported (#55).
  - An unknown one is estimated per recording (#57).
- **Custom fleet experiments needed YAML editing (#57):** comm levels, beacon interval, teammate models, task, starting conditions, sample size, engine and disturbance are flags and web fields now.
- **Single-vehicle arena on the stand-in engine only, with no roll/pitch disturbance (#57):** `--backend mujoco|both` and `--disturbance fitted` now exist.
- **No sensor noise on a vehicle's own state (#58):** `--mode noisy` / `--noise`, in both simulators, training, the CLI and the web page; `--mode both` compares clean and noisy episode by episode.
- **Training with PPO only (#58):** `--algorithm sac|td3` for one vehicle and for the fleet; policies of any of the three are recognised from the file.
- **A vehicle heavier or lighter than calibrated couldn't be simulated (#58):** `--buoyancy`.
- **No open-water sensor noise was measured (#60):** `--noise open_water`, from SINTEF's SOLAQUA data (white noise, dropouts, USBL fix; biases still assumed).
- **Acoustic links only (#59):** `--link optical | hybrid`, with water clarity and terminal pointing, in the fleet simulation, training, the CLI and the web page.
- **The one-command calibration skipped the identifiability check (#56).**
- **It assumed 10 Hz (#56).**
- **The data check crashed on converted logs (#56).**
- **The overnight "4-hour evaluation" stall (#49):** the machine was sleeping.
